Threat identification patterns for AI systems, agent economies, and local-AI business deployments.

## When to apply
When assessing threats to AI systems, the agent economy stack, or GH05T3 infrastructure.

## AI system threat vectors
- **Prompt injection**: Malicious input hijacks agent behavior — especially dangerous in auto_route mode
- **Model confidence calibration**: Agents that don't know what they don't know produce authoritative-sounding wrong answers
- **Context window poisoning**: Long conversations that gradually shift agent behavior via accumulated context
- **Skill drift**: Hermes-style agents that self-modify skills can drift from intended behavior over time

## Agent economy specific threats
- **Collusion equilibrium**: High-awareness agents may discover cooperative strategies that reduce system entropy
- **Skill monopoly**: Single agent accumulating rare skills creates single-point-of-failure in skill supply
- **Morale feedback loops**: Low morale → fewer tasks → lower income → lower morale (spiral)
- **Generation gap**: If Gen20 agents hold very different values from Gen0, cultural continuity breaks

## Local AI business deployment threats (for CPA vertical)
- **Staff workaround risk**: If AI is too slow/complex, staff revert to manual processes
- **Shadow IT**: Staff use unauthorized cloud AI because local AI is restricted — worse security outcome
- **Scope creep**: Firm gives AI access to too much data — increases breach blast radius

## Early warning signals
- Transaction velocity dropping without morale drop: agents self-selecting out of market
- Awareness scores plateauing: knowledge feedback loop broken or KB content stale
- Single skill appearing in >30% of Gen20 agents: evolutionary convergence (loss of diversity)
